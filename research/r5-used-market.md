## Market context 2025-2026 (sourced)

- **Memory cost surge (fact, fetched).** TrendForce (2 Feb 2026) raised its 1Q26 forecast for conventional DRAM contract prices to +90-95% QoQ (from 55-60%), NAND Flash to +55-60% QoQ (from 33-38%), and PC DRAM to more than +100% QoQ. The article does not split DDR4 from DDR5. [17]
- **Further increases (snippet).** A later TrendForce-based report put Q2 2026 DRAM contract prices at +58-63% QoQ and NAND at +70-75%, driven by AI demand for high-capacity server memory. Snippet only, not opened. [19]
- **NAND wafer costs (snippet).** Tom's Hardware reported NAND wafer costs up about 25% in February 2026 as spot prices climbed again. Snippet only. [18]
- **Slowing or reversing in Europe (snippet, conflicting).** Tom's Hardware reported retail DDR5 prices slowly dropping in Europe, and a spring 2026 tracker said US prices dipped about 20% then reversed through summer. Direction is not settled. [23][22]
- **Used-market knock-on (snippet).** Used legacy AM4 CPUs and DDR4 demand rose: Tom's Hardware (via a mirror, undated) reported used Ryzen 7 5800X3D at up to about $800, above a new 9800X3D. [22]
- **GPU supply and demand (snippet).** Jon Peddie Research, as reported by heise, put Q-on-Q graphics card sales just under 11.5 million units, down 4.5%, with a roughly 10% decline expected for 2026. Nvidia reportedly will not launch a new desktop generation until 2028. Snippet only; the heise URL I tried returned 404. [21]
- **Tariffs (snippet).** Tom's Hardware reported that the 25% Section 301 tariff on Chinese-made electronics, including GPUs and motherboards, was pushed from 29 Nov 2025 to **10 Nov 2026**. I found no reporting on what happens on or after that date. Verify on USTR or the Federal Register before relying on it. [20]
- **Used GPU prices tracked on eBay (summary).** Most 2026 sources agree used high-end NVIDIA prices rose from spring to autumn 2026 (e.g. RTX 3090 from about $700-850 in March to about $1,200-1,600 in autumn across trackers). The spread between trackers is wide, so treat the trend as medium confidence and the levels as low.

## Refresh method

1. **Sold versus asking.** Use **eBay Sold Items** (completed, sold filter) as the only price basis for "used price". Asking prices run higher (one snippet says 20-40%; this is unverified, cite as a rough guess). Label any asking-based range as "asking" in the table.
2. **Trackers.** ResalePrices, GPUDojo, and similar trackers mix asking and sold in the same page. Record the tracker's own wording and the listing count it states. Do not count them as sold data unless the tracker states it.
3. **Minimum sample.** Proposed rule (inference, not sourced): at least 10 sold items within the last 60 days before publishing a range. Below 10, mark the row "low" and say so.
4. **Outliers.** Drop the top and bottom 10% of sold prices, and any listing with "for parts", "not working", "untested", bundles, or systems. ResalePrices says it filters these; its filter rules are not documented in detail.
5. **Period.** Use only sales from the last 60 days for the headline range. Show the older median as context.
6. **Shipping.** Compare shipped prices with shipped prices. Record whether shipping is included (GPUDojo P40 figures include shipping; Amazon figures may not).
7. **Region.** Price EU and US separately. Do not convert USD to EUR. Use local sold data (eBay.de sold, Kleinanzeigen closed listings where accessible). Note that eBay US returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch in this session; do not work around that.
8. **Date stamp.** Every row needs: region, currency, source URL, date, sample size, basis.
9. **Re-check cadence.** Monthly, or weekly for GPUs during rapid moves (the 3080 and 3090 moved 10-25% in 30-90 days per trackers).

## Test checklist by part type

### GPU
- [ ] Identify the model with **GPU-Z**: name, GPU device ID, BIOS version, memory size, memory type. Compare against the seller's photos and the listing. (Tool-level detail not verified in this session; GPU-Z is standard practice.)
- [ ] Check the BIOS version against the vendor's release list. Note any "unknown BIOS" mismatch.
- [ ] **VRAM error test**: `memtest_vulkan` from the official repo (github.com/GpuZelenograd/memtest_vulkan). Standard test is 5 minutes; wait at least 6 minutes, then stop with Ctrl+C. Rare errors may need 2-3 hours. Any reported error means "the PC has hardware problems"; the README notes it is hard to tell VRAM chips from the GPU or memory controller. Some AMD cards show low load; try toggling Resizable BAR. [24]
- [ ] Stress test for stability and temperature (for example OCCT or a game benchmark). Watch for crashes, artifacts, and clock throttling. (Tool choice not verified here.)
- [ ] **Temperatures.** Read GPU core, hotspot (junction), and memory junction temperatures under load.
  - AMD: RDNA4 BIOS limits of 110 C for edge and hotspot, and 108 C (Hynix) or 105 C (Micron) memory. These are throttle limits, not recommended sustained values. [25] For RX 5700-series, AMD said a 110 C hotspot in gaming was within spec. [26] AMD has not published a memory junction spec; treat that as a gray area. [25]
  - NVIDIA: no single limit found. NVIDIA distinguishes target, slowdown, maximum operating and shutdown temperatures per model. Read the exact figure from the model's spec or the driver data. GDDR6X limits of 95 C or 105 C are from one unverified forum post. [25]
  - Rule of thumb (low-quality source): memory junction above about 100 C during gaming points to degraded pads or paste. [27]
- [ ] **Fans.** Listen for bearing noise (click, grind, rattle). Check smooth fan speed scaling. Dual-ball bearings are more durable than sleeve bearings (general guides, low confidence). [27]
- [ ] **Physical signs.** Discoloured or burned PCB or backplate around VRM, bent heatsink fins, loose screws, burn marks or bent pins on the PCIe connector, yellowed or compressed thermal pads. [27]
- [ ] **Mining history.** Ex-mining cards are not automatically bad, but check fans, pads, backplate and power-connector wear. Ask whether the card has been opened or repasted (repaste/repad is maintenance, but can hide past overheating). Budget about $20-40 for paste and pads (single low-quality source). [27]
- [ ] **Warranty transfer.** Check the brand policy in writing before buying. Example: Corsair's lifetime warranty does not transfer to secondhand buyers (Engadget, for RAM; GPU brands not checked). [15]

### CPU
- [ ] Check every pin (AM4/AM5) with a light and magnifier. Bent pins are a repeated buyer complaint in deal threads (low-confidence source). [14]
- [ ] Check the socket and IHS for scratches, discoloration, or heat marks.
- [ ] Load test for at least 15-30 minutes and watch temperatures and throttling. (Standard practice; no source fetched in this session.)
- [ ] Delidding signs: a lifted or re-glued IHS, uneven TIM residue, or a seal mark. (No source found in this session; inference.)

### RAM
- [ ] **MemTest86** (paid/official) or **Memtest86+** (free). They are separate products. Boot from USB, not inside Windows. [29]
- [ ] Test one stick at a time, at least 4 passes per stick (one guide says 8 passes overnight). Any error = fail. [29]
- [ ] **TestMem5** on Windows for XMP/EXPO overclock stability, after a default-speed pass. [29]
- [ ] Check the XMP/EXPO profile, and the module markings (IC type, PCB) against the claim.
- Used RAM is generally considered lower risk than a PSU; testing before use is still recommended. [30]

### Motherboard
- [ ] Confirm the BIOS version and that the board boots and POSTs with the CPU and memory listed.
- [ ] Check socket pins for bent or missing pins, and check the VRM heatsinks for looseness.
- [ ] Check the SATA and PCIe slots, and USB ports. (No source fetched in this session; inference.)

### SSD
- [ ] **CrystalDiskInfo** (SMART health, power-on hours, and total host writes). Compare host writes to the rated TBW. (Tool details not verified here.)
- [ ] **Fake capacity**: **h2testw** (write + verify; overwrites the whole drive; can take hours) or **ValiDrive** (fast sampling check). Only run on a drive whose contents you can overwrite. [28]
- [ ] A drive priced under 60% of the lowest legitimate retailer is suspect (one low-quality source; treat as a heuristic, not a rule). [28]

### PSU
- [ ] No easy consumer test; a dedicated tester is needed for a proper check. [30]
- [ ] Age and history are unknown and matter most. Electrolytic capacitors age with heat, regardless of how many hours the unit has run. [30]
- [ ] Most guides recommend buying new PSUs. Opinions differ (one forum view says roughly 3-year-old quality units can be a good buy). [30]
- [ ] Warranty is usually not transferable. [30]

## Listing red flags

- Generic or stock photos, or a GPU-Z screenshot that does not match the listed model or serial.
- Price far below the sold-price range for the last 60 days. Use the table as a sanity check, not a substitute for sold data.
- Refusal of an in-person test, or of a test with the seller's PC or the buyer's PC.
- Payment or deposit demanded off-platform, or a "friends and family" payment request.
- Heavy wording about "for parts", "untested", "not working", or "as is" with a full price.
- "Mining card, works fine" with no photos of the fans, backplate, or power connector.
- "Warranty" claimed without proof of purchase or a transfer policy.
- Bundles and systems that hide the GPU's true price.
